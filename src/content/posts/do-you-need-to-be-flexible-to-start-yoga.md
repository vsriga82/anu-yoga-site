No — and I wish I could say that quietly to every person who has decided yoga
isn't for them because they can't reach their toes.

It comes up in almost every first conversation. "I'm too stiff." "Maybe once
I've loosened up a little." Said gently, almost as an apology, as though
flexibility were the price of admission and they'd arrived without it.

But it was never the price. Flexibility is what a practice slowly gives you. It
was never what the practice asked for.

## The myth in the mirror

Somewhere yoga became a picture of a body folded into an impossible shape. It's
easy to look at that and quietly close the door on yourself.

The practice I know is a different thing. It doesn't ask what your body can
already do. It asks only that you meet it where it is today, and offer it a
little more ease than it had yesterday. Stiffness isn't a failing. It's simply
where you happen to begin — and every beginning is a real one.

## You begin with the body you have

This is truest for the people who feel furthest from it — the back that
complains, the shoulder that never quite forgave, the years at a desk, the
condition carried carefully through each day.

That body isn't set aside until it's ready. That body is where we begin. A shape
can be made smaller, held by a chair, softened until it fits. Nothing is forced.
If a movement doesn't belong to you today, we find one that does. The practice
bends toward you — never the reverse.

## What softening looks like

People imagine the first session will ask a great deal of them. It usually asks
the opposite.

More often it is sitting, and breathing, and noticing. Small movements, well
within reach. Learning where the body holds on, and what it feels like to let a
little of it go. It happens in inches, not leaps — a breath a little deeper in
week three than in week one. That noticing is the beginning of the healing.

## Flexibility comes on its own

And here is the quiet part. When you stop treating flexibility as something to
earn first, and simply practise, it arrives anyway — not because you chased it,
but because you kept returning with patience. With it come the things you
actually came for. Easier sleep. Less holding. A body that feels a little more
like home.

You never had to earn your place here. You only had to begin.
